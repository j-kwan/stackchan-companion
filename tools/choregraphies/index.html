    Off. Turning it on RELEASES the servos: the head goes limp and you place it by hand.

    Timing references: snappy move 80-150 ms · normal gesture 250-400 ms · slow slide 500-700 ms · expressive hold ≥ 600 ms beyond the travel.

    Preview

    yaw 0° end stop pitch 0° end stop gaze 0

    Drag on the head to set the servos: horizontally the yaw, vertically the pitch. The sliders and the CSV follow. Travel downwards is short — pitch only goes to +6° — and “end stop” lights up as soon as you reach it: that is the real servo’s limit, not the tool’s.

    The eyes replay the firmware’s chain: preset per eye (12 emotions are asymmetric), the flips of EyeRig::mirrored, the invariants of normalize, EyeDrawer tracing corner radius by corner radius, and the real color of the expression (emotionToRgb, dimmed by eye_color_dim). The special renders are in there too: the star of Excited, the cross of Dead, the blushing cheeks, the sparkles and the sweat drop. The data is extracted from the sources, nothing is copied by hand.
    What is not reproduced: idle animations (breathing, saccades, micro-overshoot), VOR, dynamic preset variants (Sad turning into Scary when looking up) and the CRT effect. The band below the eyes is only a framing reference: a reminder that the screen is 240 px tall and the eyes take up just 160 of them. The tool shows the pose of a keyframe, not the life of the robot around it.

    CSV — /dances/<name>.csv on the SD card

    Editable directly: the timeline updates on every valid keystroke. The file name gives the dance its name. Columns: yaw,pitch,servoMs,holdMs,emotion,lid,gazeY.
